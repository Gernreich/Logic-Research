# The letter, transcribed

Shea Zellweger to the author, postmarked Canton, Ohio, 17 February 2006
(`scans/page-01.jpg` to `page-03.jpg`). Two handwritten pages in black, with
notes in red, blue, green and orange. Line breaks follow the page; spelling is
as written, with `[sic]` where it differs from the usual form.

In his handwriting Zellweger writes the glyphs as letters. Here they use the
same stand-ins as the rest of this folder: **c** = A, **ɔ** = not A, **u** = B,
**d** = and, **h** = not-and, **ч** = or, **Ի** = A implies B (his letter is a
vertical flip of h, which Unicode lacks), **x** = the tautology. **N** in
"NA" is the prefix *not*.

## What it says

Page 1 is modus ponens done the textbook way: to get anywhere you must first
recall that A ⊃ B means not-A or B, and substitute.

Page 2 does it in the Logic Alphabet, where each glyph *is* its truth table,
so the variables can be written as glyphs too (c for A, u for B) and the
whole formula computed on shapes:

* **c Ի u gives Ի.** Rewriting A ⊃ B as not-A or B (flip c to ɔ, then
  overlay ɔ and u keeping every stem) only rebuilds the Ի you started with:
  the "needless side show".
* **Ի d c gives d.** Overlay the two and keep the stems they share.
* **d Ի u is x.** Writing it as h ч u, the overlay of h and u has every stem.
  Or at a glance: d's one stem lies among the two stems of u, which is what
  implication means.
