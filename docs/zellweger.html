Zellweger's Logic Alphabet

Ten rules of inference worked in Shea Zellweger's script for the sixteen two-variable functions, and his 2006 letter about doing modus ponens that way. Every line is checked here on every row of its truth table.

The script is Shea Zellweger's. Zellweger (1925–2022), who chaired psychology at Mount Union College in Alliance, Ohio, created the Logic Alphabet: a letter for each of the sixteen binary connectives, shaped so that flipping or turning a letter performs the matching logical operation. The glyphs, their shapes and their names are his. So is the handwritten letter.

The derivations are this repository's author's. The ten typed sheets take Zellweger's script and work common rules of propositional logic in it, from the rule down to x, in a colour glyph font of the author's with the operators in red.

How the script works

Each glyph stands on a little square of four places, one for each row of the truth table: A and B both true, A true and B false, A false and B true, both false. A stem stands where the function is true. So a glyph is its truth table, and A and B themselves can be written as glyphs (c and u). Then a whole formula can be computed on shapes: a left–right flip negates, overlaying two glyphs and keeping every stem gives or, keeping only the stems they share gives and. A formula that comes out as x, a stem in every place, is a tautology.

x1111tautology: always true
d1000A and B
h0111not (A and B)
ч1110A or B
p0001neither A nor B
Ի1011A implies B
q0100A and not B
c1100A
ɔ0011not A
u1010B
n0101not B

Squares read TT TF / FT FF. The glyphs are set here in the nearest Unicode letters: Ի stands in for Zellweger's implication letter, a vertical flip of his h, and ч for his OR. On the sheets, and below, a letter with a tilde (Ã) is not A: once the variables are letters rather than glyphs there is nothing to flip, so the tilde carries the negation. That lets every operator but and and or drop away, and in the last lines each bracket names one row of the truth table. The rule holds when the brackets cover every row.

The letter, 17 February 2006

Zellweger does modus ponens twice: the textbook way, which needs you to remember that A ⊃ B means not-A or B, and then in his script, where c Ի u gives back Ի (“comes right back, needless side show”), Ի d c gives d, and d Ի u is plainly x, because d's one stem lies among the two stems of u. Transcription.

The ten sheets

Each rule is worked four ways where it has two variables (compressed and expanded, infix and postfix) and two ways where it has three or four. The compressed lines compute on glyphs, and the steps under them show what each operation gives. The expanded lines rewrite every operator as and, or and tildes, then spell out the rows. All 130 lines were parsed and evaluated on every row: 128 are tautologies as printed, and two have a slip in the small print (marked), each a tautology once corrected. The Modus Tollens sheet has its headings one notation off; the true notation is shown with the printed one beside it.
